**Figure. Sensitivity and stability of the frozen models across input conditions, sites, and random seeds.** **(A)** For Q1, ΔNADE is the change in normalized average displacement error when only the most recent 0.30 s of history is available, relative to the full history. For Q2, ΔNADE is the change when observation delay increases from 0.10 to 0.20 s. Both analyses use the same 212 development windows and 390 previously evaluated test windows. The Q3 heatmap shows the weighted on-time rate for nine service-duration settings, αd + β, where d is the sampled source duration. Each cell uses the same 300 duration seeds and weights the three cases equally. **(B)** With 0.30 s of history, requiring a gap of at least 2 s between successive prediction windows for the same hand within a recording leaves 308 windows. Doubling the delay increases NADE at all eight previously evaluated sites. Circles denote development sites and diamonds denote test sites. **(C)** Points show the mean difference in weighted on-time rate between long and nominal durations (long minus nominal). Horizontal bars show 95% paired bootstrap intervals from 10,000 resamples of duration seeds. The multiplier α increases from 1 to 1.5 with β fixed at 0.5 s. The first 30 seeds in the expanded batch are included in the full set of 300. **(D)** For case S2 under nominal durations, the lower-tail mean is the mean weighted on-time rate among the lowest-scoring 10% of runs. In the initial split, the lowest 15 of 150 runs in each half gave means 8.89 percentage points apart. In two new batches, the lowest 30 of 300 runs per batch gave means 0.28 percentage points apart, within the prespecified 5-point tolerance. **(E)** For each Q3 duration setting, tasks and realized durations were held fixed for the same 90 instances. The original and replication batches each used 20 distinct internal seeds. From left to right, the plots show the mean weighted on-time rate, the mean of the within-instance sample standard deviations, and the proportion of seed pairs yielding the same first action, averaged across instances. Within each pair of points, the original batch is on the left. SD denotes standard deviation and pp denotes percentage points.
